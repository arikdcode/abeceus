# Working Premise

The rough sketch of the game's plot, motivation structure, and themes. This is a **working document** — the shape is agreed, most specifics are still open. Decisions we're reasonably committed to are marked **Decided**; things still in flux are collected under [[plot/working-premise#Open Questions|Open Questions]].

> **Format note:** This is being developed as a **video game first**, with a possible later port to the RollHub VTT / the [[game/game-design-document|tactical milsim system]]. That framing matters: the story leans on *authored* moments (the tutorial betrayal, scripted reveals, witnessed consequences) that a video game can guarantee and a tabletop game can only hope for.

> **Companion doc:** [[plot/campaign-structure|Campaign Structure]] covers *how you move through the campaign* — the information-driven location topology, act structure, and mission model. This doc is *what the story is*; that one is *how you traverse it*.

---

## One-Line Pitch

A disposable rookie is used as the fall guy for a political assassination, left to die, and survives — and to stop being hunted he has to reverse-engineer the hidden org chart of the people who threw him away, getting his revenge and his answers one rung at a time, while the world quietly teaches him there was never a clean enemy to begin with.

---

## The Opening (Decided in shape)

### The tutorial is complicity

You start as a **promising but green commando** — real potential, big ego, no connections. You get embedded in an elite special-operations squad and can't believe your luck; you think it's the reward for your talent. The first mission is the tutorial: you control these badass operators, learn the systems, feel like the shit.

The mission is a **political assassination.** You're told the target is an enemy — dangerous, deserving. You take the target out.

Then the squad sets you up. You take the fall. You hear them **laughing at you with contempt** right before you're captured. The men you looked up to disown you. Your own side recasts you publicly as a traitor.

> The tutorial's real job isn't teaching controls — it's teaching the **epistemics of the whole game**: you acted on someone else's framing of a human being, and it was a lie, and now you have to carry the body. Everything afterward is re-learning that lesson at higher and higher resolution. The assassination is the game's thesis statement (see [[plot/working-premise#The character-read bait-and-switch|character-read bait-and-switch]]).

### The prison ship (survival horror pivot)

You're meant to be quietly killed in prison — framed by law, then removed so you can't talk. A paid inmate is supposed to do it. It never happens: **Oziri pirate raiders** ([[locations/oziri/index|Oziri]]) hit the ship to break out one of their own, and in the chaos you get your chance.

The tone snaps from choreographed elite violence to **scrounging desperation.** The ship is dying — hull breaches, failing oxygen, reactors going critical, claxons. Every prisoner is loose, arming up, fighting over dwindling space and resources like rats on a sinking ship. You move section to section, and **each new section is still fucked** — you never catch your breath. Relentless pressure to survive five more minutes and reach the next compartment.

Along the way you pick up the **NPCs who become your squad** (see [[plot/working-premise#The Companions|Companions]]), reach a final compartment, and escape (escape pod / stolen craft). The wreck plausibly drifts toward or is taken to Oziri, dropping you into the slums of [[locations/oziri/zirta/index|Zirta]] — act two happens in a district we've already mapped.

## Core Motivation: Survival That Becomes the Hunt (Decided)

The spine is **survival**, in the *inescapable-deadline* sense (cf. Cyberpunk 2077's chip), **not** the immediate flee-the-explosion sense.

- The people who framed you didn't find your body. They know you're alive. **They will not stop.** You are hunted.
- Survival is **purer than revenge** as a driver, because revenge is *renounceable* (a player can decide they're done) and a death sentence is not. Nearly everyone fights to live.
- Because the threat is attached and can't be outrun, survival is **active, not passive** — the only vector to safety runs *forward, into the conspiracy.* To stop being prey you must find the source.

### Survival transmutes into revenge (they coexist)

1. **Survive the next five minutes** (prison ship).
2. **Survive the week** — a marked man, a convicted traitor, hunted; staying alive requires the underbelly, contacts, resources.
3. **The hunt never ends until you end its source** — so survival *becomes* a hunt of your own.

Revenge is real and cathartic, but it rides on top of survival rather than being the base. You genuinely hate these people and want them dead; you *also* have no choice but to deal with them.

### Hunter and hunted (Decided)

You are **both.** Sometimes you initiate; sometimes *they* come for you — ambushes, scouting, watching your back. This is load-bearing, not flavour:
- It keeps "they will never stop" viscerally true session-to-session.
- It preserves **underdog vulnerability against your own power creep** — you keep feeling like prey even as you become a predator. Pure revenge (always you initiating) drifts into a power fantasy; being hunted counteracts that.

## The "Nobody" Frame (Decided)

You are **not special.** That is the point, and the engine of the whole thing.

- You were pickable *precisely because* you were disposable — a promising rookie nobody would protect, plausibly present on the op, with no ties to anyone who'd stand up for you.
- Being nothing is **more galling** than being chosen. You wanted to serve; you were used and thrown away by men who laughed.
- Separate **who you were** (a nobody — keep this pure) from **why the world keeps circling back** (acquired, not innate): you're a *loose end* whose continued existence is inconvenient, and later, useful leverage to others.

### Why frame you at all — "kill two birds" (Decided direction)

Blaming your own rookie wouldn't buy the organization distance (the CIA-can't-blame-its-own-trainee problem). So they don't. They **scrub your affiliation and recast you as something you never were** — a foreign agent / defector / member of an organization someone wants damaged.

The hit therefore does *two* jobs: **silences the target**, and **implicates a third party** by attaching you to them. Your victimhood isn't a byproduct — it's a second objective. The scrubbing is the extra twist of the knife: your country replaces the you that existed with an enemy.

### Why anyone bothers with you afterward (Decided)

Most powerful people have use for **a man of violence with nothing to lose and no attribution** — deniable, skilled, motivated, disposable. You're useful *and* still a pawn: a hired gun who can be pointed at almost anyone and owned by no one. Expect to be **double-crossed repeatedly** — it's a genuinely dodgy world where trust is hard for good reason (not as a gimmick, as its nature).

## The Conspiracy: A Hidden Org Chart (Decided structure)

You are **reverse-engineering a hierarchy.** This is the concrete mental model that keeps the fractal intrigue *legible* — the failure mode of nested-conspiracy stories is illegibility, and a chart is a mystery with structure the player can hold. Consider an in-world artifact for it (case file, contact who assembles it, a wall of connected faces).

Each node you reach delivers **three payoffs at once**:
1. **Intel** — climb the chart.
2. **A threat neutralized** — survival.
3. **A new, closer personal stake** — re-tightens the wound.

### The shape: a leaderless peer network behind an airgap (Decided)

Two layers, and separating them resolves the tension between *catharsis* (needs faces to kill) and *theme* (no one at the top):

- **The obfuscation layer — [[organizations/aia|NEXUS]] as the airgap.** NEXUS is *not* the conspiracy; it's the deniable laundering layer between the conspirators and their hands. Someone with real power leans on someone with a NEXUS connection; a work order enters the cloud, bounces through mutually-decoupled cells, and comes out as an instruction to a wetwork squad that has no idea who ordered it or why. NEXUS *can't* have a puppet master — that's the point; it's what makes "trace it back to me" structurally impossible. Its canonical **elimination-work-order** mechanic doubles as the **hunt engine** (anyone can be a deputized operative collecting a bounty on you), and its **information-bidding** market is a canonical way to *buy* leads.
- **The conspirators — a leaderless peer network.** Above/outside the cloud sits the **Immortality Consortium**: a small inner circle of powerful peers with **no single head**. There may be a facilitator/fixer who *connects* them, but not a king. This is the purest form of the theme — a system with no head to cut off — and it makes the climax a **choice, not a final boss** (see [[plot/working-premise#The Two Tracks and the Final Choice|Final Choice]]).

You **cross the airgap** to get from the deniable hands out to the first "real" person, then climb the peer network. The faces you reach are real, culpable, killable — catharsis intact — but wounding the network never *beheads* it, because it has no head. The system grinds on. You were fighting the specific limb that closed around your throat.

### Canonical grounding: Azure Reach & the Immortality Consortium (Decided direction)

The deepest layer maps onto existing canon — [[timeline/azure-reach-incident|The Azure Reach Incident]]: a rogue [[organizations/aia|AIA]] cell's bioweapon, covered up by blaming [[locations/oziri/index|Oziri]] (**a war founded on a lie**), whose anti-aging side effect now drives a cross-national **Immortality Consortium**. National allegiances mean nothing beside eternal life — that *is* the "teams are fake at the top" theme, pre-written into the setting.

- **The target** (illustrative): a whistleblower — official, scientist, or Freehold journalist — about to crack Azure Reach open, sold to you as an Oziri/enemy collaborator. The kill silences the leak; framing you as *their* agent discredits the faction that might expose the truth and pre-poisons any future leak as "enemy propaganda." Personal ruin and master cover-up are the *same act*.
- **Consortium size:** a killable **inner circle** (a handful who know everything; a few heavily characterized) wrapped in a larger **penumbra** of complicit beneficiaries who don't know the full shape. Manageable revenge target *plus* the "it's the whole elite class" feeling.
- **The grayness:** their *goal* — not wanting to die — is the most relatable human fear; their *means* — mining a quarantine of the permanently insane, murdering witnesses for decades — is monstrous. The gap is the grayness.
- **[[timeline/azure-reach-incident|Valen Morrec]] — the detonator and the dark mirror** (Decided direction): *not* a conspirator. A man discarded by the same machine, decades further down the revenge road — **your possible future, already arrived** (the "becoming the monster" arc personified). He has (or can produce) proof of the whole thing and is about to escape and release it. Your climb-from-inside and his blow-the-wall-from-outside are on a collision course; he becomes the fulcrum of the final choice.

### The reveal discipline (Decided)

- **Zoom out on the world; zoom in on the wound.** Every reveal makes the conspiracy *bigger* and the stakes *more personal* — never the reverse. Widen the lens on the machine; tighten the lens on the pain, together, every time.
- **Additive, not subtractive.** A reveal must never say "your last target didn't matter, your effort was wasted." It says "that target was real — *and* getting here uncovered something worse and closer." Recontextualize what you did; occasionally implicate *you*; never retroactively make it pointless.
- **Re-contract, don't bait-and-yank.** Each act, *deliver* a real catharsis (a genuine kill, a genuine answer) and in the delivery expose a bigger, closer stake the player *chooses* to chase. Keep every promise while widening the frame. (The opposite — reveals that only open and never close — is the mystery-box death, e.g. *Lost*.)

### Example ladder (illustrative, not final)

You catch **one** wetwork operative → interrogate → a NEXUS cell → another cell → **out of the cloud** (crossing the airgap) → a low-level conspirator who actually knows *something* → up the peer network toward the inner circle. Six-or-seven jumps, each one *several* missions (cultivate a faction, acquire gear/allies, run side objectives), not a single step. The pettiness (deniable operatives who don't even know what they're doing) opens into system-defining stakes; you were several layers down, one minor implication of something vast.

## Theme (Decided)

**There is no central villain; the system incentivizes inhuman behavior, and ordinary people get ground up in the works.**

- Good-guys-vs-bad-guys is a **story sold to the lower classes.** At the top, teams are **fluid and transactional** — supposed enemies collaborate when it profits them. The escalation of the plot isn't "a bigger boss"; it's **"the categories you believed in are fake."**
- The player is the **flashlight, not the treasure.** Every layer peeled back is about *the world*, not about how special you are — which lets the web expand indefinitely.

### The vigilante option and "becoming the monster" (Decided as *option*, not core)

- Straightforward vigilantism as a *sincere, endorsed* motive is a trap (edgelord fantasy). Rorschach/Batman work because their stories **interrogate** them.
- So vigilante action is **available, not the thesis** — the underbelly is full of innocents caught in the works, and you *can* take matters into your own hands.
- The honest counterweight is the axis **vigilante ↔ succumbing to the system / becoming the thing you hated.** Revenge is the perfect solvent for ethics ("just this once, to get closer"). The corruption should be **disguised as pragmatism**, never presented as an obvious evil choice — the player should look back and be unable to find the moment they crossed the line.

## Characterization Philosophy (Decided)

- **Normal distribution of morality.** A few genuinely good people, a few genuine monsters, **most people in the vast middle** — comprehensible even when they disgust you. Avoid *both* cartoons: clean good/evil **and** "everyone's equally scum" grey mush (adolescent cynicism, equally flat). You need real innocents (so stakes are real) and a few true monsters (so the world has a floor).
- **Monsters indict the system.** The point of a genuine monster isn't that he's evil — it's that the world **produced, protects, or celebrates** him. The ideal is the **publicly celebrated "hero"**: a decorated figure of the [[setting/oziri-conflict|Oziri Conflict]] whose fame rests on the Azure Reach lie — not cartoonishly evil, but **put on a pedestal for it.** (This public hero is a *conspirator*, distinct from the clandestine wetwork squad — see [[plot/working-premise#The clandestine squad vs. the public hero|the squad/hero split]].) Even he gets a *causal* backstory (e.g. war normalized from a young age) — explaining, not exculpating.
- **Everyone has a lie they tell themselves.** For each significant character, know the story they tell to stay okay with themselves — nobody is the villain of their own narrative. The most chilling have the most *coherent* self-justifications; the most tragic have ones that are almost right. **The rationalization the game is really about is the player's own** — the "becoming the monster" arc is just the player's accumulated "just this once," and the player supplies that inner monologue themselves.

### The character-read bait-and-switch (Decided)

First impressions are systematically incomplete; fuller truth is *discoverable* by the player who uses their brain. The assassination target is the **first instance**: told he's a monster, you later learn it wasn't the whole truth, and you have to grapple with having killed him on a lie.

**Cut both ways.** Sometimes the "monster" you were warned about is human; sometimes the decent-seeming person is worse than you assumed. If every reveal runs "actually they're nicer than you thought," players learn the pattern and it curdles into sentimentality. Keep the direction unpredictable.

## Consequences: Witnessed, Not Scored (Decided)

**No morality slider. No karma. No weighted scales** (evil = richer but hated / good = poor but loved). Morality is never a game mechanic.

Distinguish three things usually lumped together:
1. **Karmic weighting** — the game grades you and pays it out. **Rejected.**
2. **Causal consequence** — the world reacts *truthfully*: you kill a man and watch his wife sob over the body; a spared enemy remembers; a burned contact closes a door. **Embraced.** The consequence of a choice is that you and the world must *live in its wake* — you have to carry it, with no number telling you whether to feel good or bad.
3. **Arbitrary / unfair outcome** — good punished, evil rewarded, sometimes at random. **Seasoning only.**

> **Critical caution on #3:** randomness only means something against a *causal baseline*. If outcomes are frequently decoupled from choices, the player learns their agency is fake and disengages — the opposite of "live with your decisions." The world must be **mostly causally legible** so the *rare* unfair outcome lands as tragedy (cf. *Chinatown*) instead of noise. Baseline = actions ripple truthfully; unfairness = the deliberate, occasional violation that says "the world has no karma."

Choices affect the **plot and the fiction** (causality, who lives, which doors open), never a meter. The game is **adult and unsanitized** — the real consequences of violence are shown, not laundered (contrast *Fable*'s consequence-free town massacres). It does not tell you what to think; it puts you in hard situations and makes you decide and *live with it.*

## The Companions (Decided in shape)

- **Loyal to you, not necessarily good.** Not "secretly good people" — people who genuinely have your back while not being good overall.
- **Shared glue: they're all loose ends.** Each is someone the system spat out, wrote off, or would rather forget — disowned, like you, though not necessarily framed like you. This explains the military competence (ex-soldiers, ex-clan enforcers, ex-corporate security who know too much) and gives the crew a real identity: **an island of the deniable.**
- **Not all bound to you the same way.** Some relationships are tense; some have motivations that pull against yours. Earn trust in *both* directions; at least one should be genuinely dangerous or serving another master.
- **They are the emotional ballast for a faceless threat.** You can't hate a system, but you can grieve what it *costs* — and it costs specific people you travelled with. The crew is also the **mirror**: they see your corruption before you can. The darkest version — one companion is drifting the same way you are, and you see in him what you can't see in yourself.
- **First companion** should save your ass in a tight spot on the prison ship, establishing *earned* trust, not scripted trust.

## The Two Tracks and the Final Choice (Decided in shape)

Run two motives on **separate tracks** so intellectual reveals never cheapen emotional payoffs:

- **Emotional track** — the **squad who laughed at you.** Personal, concrete, doesn't care who pulled the strings. Getting to them stays satisfying no matter what you learn. Delivers *closure* at intervals.
- **Intellectual track** — the **conspiracy / org chart.** Fractures and widens; nothing is ever the final answer. Delivers *mystery.*

Because the Consortium is **leaderless**, the finale can't be "kill the apex" — there is no head. So the two tracks **converge on a choice, not a boss**, and [[timeline/azure-reach-incident|Morrec]] is the detonator that makes it concrete: you finally have your shot at the man who laughed / the celebrated hero **and** you hold (or can hand Morrec) the proof that would expose the whole arrangement — **and you can't do both.** The bullet or the truth. Personal revenge or its meaning. Likely the **first genuinely free choice** the used man ever gets — tying theme (you were never allowed to decide) to catharsis (you decide) in one beat.

> **Open:** does the protagonist even *want* vindication, or just blood? If he doesn't care about clearing his name, Morrec's proof becomes a *temptation he might refuse* — more interesting than a prize he chases.

### The clandestine squad vs. the public hero (Decided)

Two distinct antagonist roles, deliberately split — clandestine operators can't also be public celebrities:
- **The wetwork squad** — clandestine. Personal, early/mid catharsis; the men who laughed, killed in the shadows. Drives the *emotional* track.
- **The public "hero"** — a Consortium conspirator higher up, celebrated for a war built on the lie. You seethe watching him lauded while you're branded a traitor; his glory and your ruin are the same lie. Late-ladder, *thematic* hatred.
- **The bridge:** the squad leader may be the hero's instrument, so getting the squad leader is what first points you at the man on the screen.

### Faction playground

Because you're a deniable asset chasing threads, you can work with (and be used/betrayed by) many powers: [[locations/oziri/index|Oziri]] pirate clans, [[organizations/aia|Atruna intelligence]], [[locations/mesulea/index|Mesulea]] corporations, [[locations/nuilea/index|Nuilea]] military, aristocrats, and more. Missions are means to *your* ends (intel, access, resources), full of morally gray — sometimes outright black — work.

## Open Questions

- **The ending fork** (the big one — everything upstream should be tuned to land it):
  1. **Partial peace** — you sever this branch, the specific hunters are gone, you actually get to stop running, but the machine that made them is untouched. Bittersweet, earned, closed.
  2. **Never safe** — you get every face and the hunt just re-sources higher up; the freedom you fought for doesn't exist. Bleak, thematically maximal, risks invalidating the climb.
  3. **You become a node** — you end this branch and in doing so become a feared name others will one day hunt up their own chart. Survival and the monster-arc fold into one final image.
- **Who is the assassination target, and what does the kill *accomplish*?** Pick the accomplishment before the identity. The public reason is a lie; the squad's real orders aren't the whole truth either; the deepest "why" keeps expanding. Strong criterion: the lie that jails *you* should be the same lie that crowns the final-boss "hero."
- **Does the protagonist know he's becoming the monster?** Tragic *awareness* (Rorschach/Kovacs — cold) vs. *dramatic irony* (he still thinks he's the wronged hero while the player sees the slide — warmer, sadder). Sets the whole tone.
- **The squad roster** — who's the psychopath, who had moral doubts, who's indifferent, who's corrupt/in-it-for-money, who's the hyper-nationalist true believer; which one(s) are *not* simply evil (coerced / already dead / doubting) to keep revenge a *question* rather than a checklist. (The publicly celebrated "hero" is a separate, higher role — a conspirator, not the clandestine squad leader.)
- **The apex — probably none.** Leaning toward a *leaderless peer network* (no single head), which best fits the theme and makes the finale a choice, not a boss. If a single characterized face is wanted as a capstone, a **fixer/facilitator** (connector, not king) fits a headless network better than a monarch. The canonical hook — someone powerful enough to have had [[timeline/azure-reach-incident|Emperor Tashen IV]] killed — can sit *inside* the inner circle without being "the boss."
- **Which power do the inner circle and the public hero come from?** [[polities/atruna|Atruna]] is the gravity well (AIA, Azure Reach, the compromised Emperor); a most-prominent member from a supposed *enemy* power would reinforce "the teams are fake at the top."
- **Whose betrayal is it — which nation did the protagonist serve?** [[polities/atruna|Atruna]] = intimate, closest to the rot; an outsider (e.g. [[locations/nuilea/index|Nuilea]]) = climbing toward a secret that isn't even his nation's.
- **How safety is dangled** — how relentless the hunt feels, and whether real safety is ever offered, must be tuned to whichever ending fork is chosen.
